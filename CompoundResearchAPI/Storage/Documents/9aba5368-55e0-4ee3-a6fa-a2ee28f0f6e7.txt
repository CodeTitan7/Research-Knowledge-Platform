Metformin primarily lowers hepatic glucose production by activating AMP-activated protein kinase, commonly known as AMPK. This activation reduces gluconeogenesis in the liver. Metformin also has mild inhibitory effects on mitochondrial Complex I, which contributes to its metabolic effects and altered cellular energy status. Unlike sulfonylureas, metformin does not increase insulin secretion from the pancreas, and it carries a low risk of hypoglycemia when used as monotherapy. It is typically the first-line medication for managing type 2 diabetes.
